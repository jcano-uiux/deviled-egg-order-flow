## Platform

web

## Users

Everyday pickup/delivery customers ordering lunch or dinner near one of Deviled Egg Co.'s four Texas storefronts (McKinney, Denison, Rockwall, Coppell). They're choosing food for now or soon, not browsing a catalog — the flow needs to get them from "what am I craving" to a placed order quickly, for either pickup or delivery.

Two further audiences, from the live Nationwide Shipping and Catering pages and the designer & developer notes (Concept 01):
- **Gift and holiday senders** ordering deviled egg kits to ship anywhere in the contiguous US (holiday pre-orders for Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas). They plan ahead around a ship week, not a pickup slot.
- **Office and event buyers** ordering catering: protein box meals (10-box minimum), bundles that serve 10, and add-ons, from boardrooms to backyards. They order a day or more ahead, for a date, and often for other people.

## Product Purpose

Let customers browse the Deviled Egg Co. menu and place a real pickup order online: pick items, customize a Build Your Own Dozen, manage a cart, check out, and get a confirmed order with a fulfillment estimate. The same prototype also takes Nationwide Shipping orders (kits shipped cold, overnight) and Catering orders (box meals and bundles for an event), each with its own cart and checkout. Deviled Egg Co. now offers everyday delivery (owner, 2026-10-07). How a delivery order is taken, in this prototype and in production, is not decided yet and is not built: see the open decisions below.

## Positioning

Freshness and made-to-order customization are the mechanism, not a generic delivery-app menu list. The signature move is Build Your Own Dozen — choose up to 4 flavors from the full lineup — and every screen should sell "made fresh daily, your way" rather than reading like an interchangeable food-delivery clone.

## Operating Context

- Four physical storefronts fulfill pickup orders: McKinney, Denison, Rockwall, and Coppell, TX. Each has a real street address.
- Three order paths are placed in-app, each with its own cart, drawer tab and checkout page: **Pickup** (the four storefronts), **Delivery** (the same menu brought to an address near a store, built in the prototype, on this site; fee, minimum, area and courier are placeholders) and **Nationwide Shipping**. Catering is not a path or a cart of its own: a catering item goes into the Pickup or Delivery bag, with everything else in it. Deviled Egg Co. now offers everyday delivery (owner, 2026-10-07); whether the real courier is in-house or a partner is still undecided.
- Nationwide Shipping (from the live page): cold-shipped overnight to the 48 contiguous states from the Texas kitchen; orders placed Monday–Wednesday arrive within the same week; a tracking link goes out the morning of shipping; eggs are best within 5 days of arrival per the page (the kit product pages say within 3 days, an unresolved inconsistency on the live site).
- Catering (from the live order form's own data): box meals are a 10-box minimum; pickup is free and can be same-day with at least 2 hours' notice; delivery is a $20 flat fee, free over $200, needs 24–48 hours' notice, and serves within about 5 miles of a location; a team member confirms by phone within an hour.
- Texas sales tax (8.25%) applies to pickup and catering orders placed on this site. Tax on shipped orders is undecided.
- Customers already have a mental model from mainstream delivery apps (Uber Eats); the flow intentionally follows that familiar interaction pattern (menu → item customization → cart → checkout → confirmation) rather than inventing a new one, while carrying the Deviled Egg Co. brand identity throughout.

## Product Principles

1. Every screen sells freshness and flavor customization as the hero — never just a scrollable item list like a generic delivery app.
2. Pickup, Delivery, Nationwide Shipping and Catering are the order paths built in-house. Everyday Delivery's fee, minimum and area are placeholders, and how it will be fulfilled (this site's own courier or a partner) is undecided; until it is, it gets no cart and no outbound links.
3. Borrow the interaction pattern customers already know from mainstream delivery apps, but never the visual identity — every control stays on the Deviled Egg Co. gold/cream/pill design system.
4. Be honest about what's real: this prototype does not process real payments or route pickup orders to a store today, and nothing in the UI should imply otherwise.
5. Nothing is added to the bag by sending the customer to an outside page. Every product that can go in the bag — menu items and nationwide shipping kits alike — opens its own in-app modal (the same dozen-style picker, with the live product's own contents) and adds to the in-app cart. Never link to deviledeggco.com, or any other page, as the way to "Add to Bag". A delivery-partner handoff, if Delivery ends up using one, would be the one exception, because it replaces the bag rather than adding to it.
6. Carts are per fulfillment path and stay apart, with one exception: changing a checkout from pickup to delivery (or back) through the "where" modal moves that cart's lines to the other path's cart once the new path's steps are done. Carts: Pickup (the location menus), Delivery (the same order page, serving an address), Nationwide Shipping, and Catering each have their own cart, drawer tab and checkout page with that path's own rules (pickup time and store; a ship day and address; an event date, pickup or delivery, and a 10-box minimum on box meals). Delivery's own rules are an address in the delivery area, a day and time (ASAP or scheduled), a flat fee, a minimum subtotal and a courier tip.
