## Platform

web

## Users

Everyday pickup/delivery customers ordering lunch or dinner near one of Deviled Egg Co.'s four Texas storefronts (McKinney, Denison, Rockwall, Coppell). They're choosing food for now or soon, not browsing a catalog — the flow needs to get them from "what am I craving" to a placed order quickly, for either pickup or delivery.

Two further audiences, from the live Nationwide Shipping and Catering pages and the designer & developer notes (Concept 01):
- **Gift and holiday senders** ordering deviled egg kits to ship anywhere in the contiguous US (holiday pre-orders for Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas). They plan ahead around a ship week, not a pickup slot.
- **Office and event buyers** ordering catering: protein box meals (10-box minimum), bundles that serve 10, and add-ons, from boardrooms to backyards. They order a day or more ahead, for a date, and often for other people.

## Product Purpose

Let customers browse the Deviled Egg Co. menu and place a real pickup order online: pick items, customize a Build Your Own Dozen, manage a cart, check out, and get a confirmed order with a fulfillment estimate. The same prototype also takes Nationwide Shipping orders (kits shipped cold, overnight) and Catering orders (box meals and bundles for an event), each with its own cart and checkout. Deviled Egg Co. now offers everyday delivery (owner, 2026-10-07). How a delivery order is taken, in this prototype and in production, is not decided yet and is not built: see the open decisions below.

## Positioning

Freshness and made-to-order customization are the mechanism, not a generic delivery-app menu list. The signature move is Build Your Own Dozen — choose up to 4 flavors from the full lineup — and every screen should sell "made fresh daily, your way" rather than reading like an interchangeable food-delivery clone.

## Operating Context

- Four physical storefronts fulfill pickup orders: McKinney, Denison, Rockwall, and Coppell, TX. Each has a real street address.
- Three order paths are placed in-app, each with its own cart, drawer tab and checkout page: **Pickup** (the four storefronts), **Nationwide Shipping**, and **Catering**. Everyday consumer **Delivery** is now offered by Deviled Egg Co. (owner, 2026-10-07). It has no funnel, cart or checkout in the prototype yet, and whether it is taken on this site or handed to third-party platforms (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, the earlier assumption) is undecided; do not assume either.
- Nationwide Shipping (from the live page): cold-shipped overnight to the 48 contiguous states from the Texas kitchen; orders placed Monday–Wednesday arrive within the same week; a tracking link goes out the morning of shipping; eggs are best within 5 days of arrival per the page (the kit product pages say within 3 days, an unresolved inconsistency on the live site).
- Catering (from the live order form's own data): box meals are a 10-box minimum; pickup is free and can be same-day with at least 2 hours' notice; delivery is a $20 flat fee, free over $200, needs 24–48 hours' notice, and serves within about 5 miles of a location; a team member confirms by phone within an hour.
- Texas sales tax (8.25%) applies to pickup and catering orders placed on this site. Tax on shipped orders is undecided.
- Customers already have a mental model from mainstream delivery apps (Uber Eats); the flow intentionally follows that familiar interaction pattern (menu → item customization → cart → checkout → confirmation) rather than inventing a new one, while carrying the Deviled Egg Co. brand identity throughout.

Known, explicit constraint (not a gap to fill silently): this is currently a front-end-only prototype. The cart persists via `localStorage`; there is no real payment processor, no backend that routes orders to a store's POS system, and no persistent user accounts. Real payment processing, POS/order-routing integration, and account persistence are known future requirements — future work should treat them as unbuilt, not assume they exist, and should not claim real payment or fulfillment is happening today.

Order funnels: the prototype is being built funnel by funnel, and only the **Pickup funnel** is in focus now. Its path is Home ("Order pickup") → Location picker → Location details modal (the store, then a day and a time) → Pickup order page (#order) → Pickup cart → Pickup checkout → Confirmation. "Start my order" in the details modal stays disabled until a day and a time are both chosen, and nothing is preselected. Choosing a time offers ASAP first for today, while the store is open and has at least 30 minutes left; ASAP means ready in about 20–30 minutes (the owner's figure). The chosen store, day and time form the pickup session: the pickup order page cannot be opened without one (a visit without it is sent to the location picker); the products page (#menu) stays open to everyone, for SEO, it is kept for the visit, it lapses when the chosen slot passes, and placing an order ends it. The location picker belongs to whichever funnel opened it: each funnel (FUNNELS in app.js) supplies the picker's title and button words and decides what choosing a store does next, so a new funnel can reuse the picker without editing it. Delivery, Nationwide Shipping and Catering are separate funnels with their own entry points; none of them uses the picker yet, and the Home "Order delivery" tile deliberately links nowhere for now. When a pickup choice is still valid, the location picker opens on it: a "Continue your pickup order" block above the ZIP search shows the store, address, day and time with a dark **Continue to menu** (to `#order`) and a **Change time** link (the details modal on that store, prefilled, whose Update order goes on to `#order`). That store's button in every list reads **Continue** instead of "Pick up here", so the saved choice is never picked again. Store results are plain white cards with no pills and no gold: name and distance, address, one status line, and a text "Pick up here ›"; the closest store taking orders leads as a wide card with the only dark button in the list. The bottom "Pickup · store · Change" dock is gone, replaced by this block.

Open product decisions (from the designer & developer notes' "Decisions to approve" and from this build; none is settled, so future work must not treat any as fact):
- Delivery route: Deviled Egg Co. now offers delivery (owner, 2026-10-07), but how it is ordered is open. The notes propose direct website delivery via courier integration, with the marketplace handoff (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub) as the alternative; neither is confirmed, and the earlier "marketplace handoff" direction no longer stands as settled. Catering's own delivery option (above) follows the live catering form.
- Delivery radius, minimums, catering capacity, and lead times per store.
- Pack sizes, flavor limits, and shipping eligibility; real shipping rates, speeds and tax; delivery item pricing and markup (none is modeled; approve from real contracts and margins).
- Catering invoice or pay-later, lead-time and service-area enforcement, and headcount or allergen guidance.

Requirements carried from the designer & developer notes for any production build (not built here): persist fulfillment, store or address, schedule, bag and configuration in one ordering session, so Back and "Add more" keep context, and validate each item and slot when the store changes; use a real payment provider, show Apple Pay only where the device supports it, guest checkout first, marketing opt-in unchecked; track the funnel method selected → location or address confirmed → menu viewed → product viewed → add to bag → checkout → confirmed purchase, with external delivery handoffs tracked separately; restore focus on navigation and validate inventory and taxes on the server.

## Product Principles

1. Every screen sells freshness and flavor customization as the hero — never just a scrollable item list like a generic delivery app.
2. Pickup, Nationwide Shipping and Catering are the order paths built in-house so far. Everyday Delivery is now offered by Deviled Egg Co. but is not built, and how it will be ordered (this site or a partner handoff) is undecided; until it is, it gets no cart and no outbound links.
3. Borrow the interaction pattern customers already know from mainstream delivery apps, but never the visual identity — every control stays on the Deviled Egg Co. gold/cream/pill design system.
4. Be honest about what's real: this prototype does not process real payments or route pickup orders to a store today, and nothing in the UI should imply otherwise.
5. Nothing is added to the bag by sending the customer to an outside page. Every product that can go in the bag — menu items and nationwide shipping kits alike — opens its own in-app modal (the same dozen-style picker, with the live product's own contents) and adds to the in-app cart. Never link to deviledeggco.com, or any other page, as the way to "Add to Bag". A delivery-partner handoff, if Delivery ends up using one, would be the one exception, because it replaces the bag rather than adding to it.
6. Carts are per fulfillment path and never merge: Pickup (the location menus), Nationwide Shipping, and Catering each have their own cart, drawer tab and checkout page with that path's own rules (pickup time and store; a ship day and address; an event date, pickup or delivery, and a 10-box minimum on box meals). Delivery has no cart yet; how it is taken is undecided.
